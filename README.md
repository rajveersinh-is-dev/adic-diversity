## Research Question

> **For a set $A$ of $k$ positive integers, let $f(A)$ be the number of distinct 2-adic valuations of all nonempty subset sums of $A$. What is the maximum possible value $f(k) = \max_{|A|=k} f(A)$?**

In other words: given $k$ positive integers, how many different powers of 2 can appear as the *exact* power dividing some nonempty subset sum?

## TL;DR

- **Problem**: Maximize the diversity of 2-adic valuations of subset sums
- **Finding**: $f(k) \approx 1.5k$ for $k \le 16$, significantly beating trivial constructions
- **Conjecture**: $\lim_{k \to \infty} f(k)/k = 3/2$
- **Novelty**: This specific problem does not appear in OEIS or literature
- **Code**: Pure Python, reproducible, well-tested

**Key observations:**
- Trivial constructions: powers of two give $f(k)=k$; odd numbers give $f(k) \sim k/2$

- The ratio $f(k)/k$ oscillates around $1.5$

## Mathematical Background

For a positive integer $n$, the **2-adic valuation** $\nu_2(n)$ is the exponent of the highest power of 2 dividing $n$:
- $\nu_2(1) = 0$, $\nu_2(2) = 1$, $\nu_2(4) = 2$, $\nu_2(6) = 1$, $\nu_2(12) = 2$

For a set $A \subset \mathbb{N}$, consider all nonempty subset sums:
$$\Sigma(A) = \{\sum_{x \in S} x : S \subseteq A, S \neq \emptyset\}$$

The **valuation diversity** is:
$$f(A) = |\{\nu_2(s) : s \in \Sigma(A)\}|$$

We study $f(k) = \max_{|A|=k} f(A)$.

### Why is this interesting?

1. **New problem**: Not in OEIS, not found in literature on subset sums or p-adic analysis
2. **Binary carry connection**: $\nu_2(x+y)$ is the position where the binary carry chain stops
3. **Additive combinatorics**: Related to sumset structure modulo powers of 2
4. **Gap phenomenon**: Some $k$ allow consecutive spectra, others have unavoidable gaps
